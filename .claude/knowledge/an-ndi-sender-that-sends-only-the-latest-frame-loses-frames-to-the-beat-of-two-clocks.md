## An NDI sender that sends only the latest frame loses 0–27 % of a video to the beat between two equal clocks — and "frames received per second" never shows it
**Area:** projection
**Trigger:** touching the send loop of `native/engine/ndi.cpp`, any pipeline that re-clocks frames from one clock to another, or measuring the NDI output's smoothness

- The offscreen window paints at the output's rate on **Chromium's** clock; the engine sent at the same rate on the **NDI SDK's** (`clock_video`). With a triple buffer where "the latest frame wins", two paints landing in one send period overwrote one another and the next period repeated a frame. Measured 2026-11-09 with a 30 fps video at 30 fps out, receiver on the same Mac (no network): **0 % to 27 % of the video's frames lost**, different on every run — it depends on the phase between the two clocks, which drifts. Chromium itself delivered every frame (`readNew` 30/s, `readSkip` 0, decoded from the picture inside the engine): the loss was entirely between reading and sending.
- Fix: a queue (`Queue` in `ndi.cpp`, up to 3 waiting, oldest first; past that the oldest goes). Each SDK tick sends the oldest waiting frame, or the last one again when nothing new came. Same SDK clock, so receivers still see a steady rate and synthesized timecode. After it: 0.04 % mid-stream on the Mac (3 single frames in ~7200), 0 of 900 on Windows (sender and receiver on one PC). At 60 out, a 30 fps video's frames go out twice each, as they should.
- **Why earlier tests said "30 fps, fine":** they counted frames *received* per second, and the sender repeats frames at its steady rate, so that number is ~30 however broken the content is. Measure *unique content frames*: the test videos carry their frame number as a barcode (24 white/black 64-px blocks on the top row + 2 marker blocks, drawn with ffmpeg `drawbox` and `enable='mod(floor(n/2^k),2)'`), and `ndi-probe count` decodes it per received frame (new / skipped / repeated, plus the SDK's own dropped count). The maintainer's spike folder for the NDI plan has the probe, the recipe and a Playwright walk (`ndi-perf.tmp.spec.ts`).
- The first frame a new receiver gets is the SDK's kept frame, up to 500 ms old (see [the last frame to every new receiver](the-ndi-sdk-hands-its-last-frame-to-every-new-receiver.md)): a jump of up to 15 frame numbers at the very start of a measurement is that, not a loss.
